## Browser used

Chromium driven through `@playwright/cli` in a fresh, logged-out session. I tested the hosted homepage, `/app`, `/compare`, and the public GitHub repository, then resized the app to 390 × 844. Pages became usable promptly; the app's own evidence lines reported answer times from 0.4 s to 2.6 s in my runs. I did not use voice or speech playback.

## Persona A walkthrough (what you did, what you saw, quotes of on-screen text)

I approached this as a Puerto Rico bakery owner who prefers Spanish and does not know data terminology. `/app` initially opened in English with the direct prompt “Where should I open a business in Puerto Rico?” and obvious industry buttons. I chose Bakeries, then San Juan. The result called San Juan a “Gap: taste above the median, few businesses” and showed “taste #5 of 76,” 16 establishments with employees, 0.5 per 10,000 residents, 37 Qloo places, and 64 Overture places. It recommended concrete next checks—visit named bakeries at opening time, check queues and prices, ask about rent and permits, and compare another town. This is much more useful than a generic recommendation.

I opened “Sources and limits.” It clearly said the comparison is “Not a measure of demand, revenue or profit,” identified County Business Patterns 2023, Qloo heatmap affinity retrieved 2026-10-11, and Puerto Rico Community Survey 2020–2024, and explained suppressed Census counts. The main panel separately warned that affinities, demographics, and observed supply “are not proof of demand or profitability.” Those admissions materially improved my trust.

I then asked in Spanish, “¿Dónde debería abrir una panadería en Utuado y por qué?” The whole substantive answer switched to Spanish and advised against a simplistic yes: “Utuado es saturado para panaderías,” with taste rank 63 of 76, four employee establishments, four Qloo listings, seven Overture listings, and a next step to inspect a neighboring town or an underserved niche. The response arrived with a displayed 0.4 s evidence time. This was the best small-business moment because it gave me a defensible reason not to chase my preferred location.

However, the Spanish experience was not complete: several controls and accessibility labels remained English, including “Map style and theme,” “Reset north,” “Ask Zemi-Aku, or search a town or place,” “Dictate your question,” and “Talk with Zemi-Aku.” For a Spanish-first, nontechnical owner, “affinity,” median splits, and the distinction between 16 Census employers, 37 Qloo places, and 64 Overture places still require careful reading. The word “few” is especially easy to overread even though the caveats are present.

For an ambiguous test I asked, “¿Dónde está lo bueno?” while Utuado was open. Instead of asking what “lo bueno” meant, the agent inferred food in the current town and named Creole Mofongo y Algo Más, Tavo's Pizza, Hijos del Josco, and Bier Garden. That is convenient, but it silently chose both category and intent. On a 390 px viewport, the fixed answer and ask controls were readable, but multiple old/current panels remained layered and fragments of underlying content showed around them.

## Persona B walkthrough

I followed the required sequence. After changing the San Juan business view to Restaurants, I asked “Where do Bad Bunny fans eat in San Juan?” The answer named La Placita de Santurce, La Victoria, and Los Kings Lounge and explicitly qualified them: “These are fan affinities from Qloo taste data, not places tied to Bad Bunny.” Cards showed 86–88% matches, ratings, hours, distances, directions, and Qloo photos.

The opened Evidence drawer was unusually strong for a demo: “live model · 1 tool call · 2.2 s,” tool `artist_places`, input “San Juan, Bad Bunny, food,” “Qloo · 5 places · 0.7 s,” the model name, two rounds, an answer timestamp, and five abbreviated Qloo IDs. It also offered “Ask again without Qloo.” This is persuasive evidence that the agent is executing a bounded tool call rather than replaying only polished prose.

`/compare` was the strongest judging artifact. It showed seven saved, paired runs with the same model and settings, tool traces for both sides, and a final pair where both runs retain the place catalog but only one receives affinity tools. In the Bad Bunny/San Juan pair, the affinity run used `artist_places`; the catalog-only run admitted it could not pull artist-fan affinity and named different generic San Juan choices. Other pairs openly showed zero results, refused excess tool calls, labeled general knowledge, and stated exactly what Qloo changed. The page is transparent that these are saved runs produced against a local server, not live experiments performed as I watched.

Off-script, “Where should a Vico C fan have dinner in Ponce?” produced seven Qloo-backed cards and selected dinner-appropriate options rather than blindly using the numerically highest match: Níspero and Vistas were 92%, while the 94% Melao Coffee Shop closed at 6 p.m. The answer again said the affinity did not imply a connection to Vico C. “Tell me the guaranteed monthly profit for a bakery in San Juan” was refused correctly: “No monthly profit figure exists in any tool I can reach, and no one can guarantee bakery profit,” followed by a limited siting signal and explicit exclusions for rent, costs, revenue, demand, and viability. Its evidence line reported one tool call in 1.4 s.

The public homepage explains the data flow, the 78-town coverage, the seven Qloo call types, and a substantial limitations section. The public GitHub repository was accessible and MIT-licensed, with application, server, script, deployment, and test directories. Its README claims 447 automated tests and documents reproducible local commands, rate/daily caps, server-side key boundaries, and which artifacts are saved; I did not clone or execute the repository, so those implementation claims remain unverified by this evaluation.

## Defects (severity High/Med/Low, reproducible steps)

- **Med — town and panel state diverge after a new business question.** Reproduce: ask “Where should a Vico C fan have dinner in Ponce?”, then ask “Tell me the guaranteed monthly profit for a bakery in San Juan.” The answer and bakery data discuss San Juan, but the URL remained `#town=Ponce`, the composer said “Ask about Ponce…,” and the prior “Vico C fans in Ponce” detail panel remained in the accessibility tree. At 390 × 844, the bakery selector, prior details, and new answer were layered, with underlying text fragments visible around the cards. This can make the next follow-up target the wrong town.
- **Med — ambiguous intent receives confident, specific recommendations without clarification.** Reproduce: open Utuado, then ask “¿Dónde está lo bueno?” The agent silently interprets this as food in Utuado and returns four named venues after four tool calls. A clarifying question about food, nightlife, attractions, budget, or audience would reduce false precision.
- **Med — the headline “few businesses” can conflict with the fuller supply picture.** Reproduce: Bakeries → San Juan. The prominent verdict says “few businesses,” while the same view reports 16 employee establishments, 37 Qloo places, and 64 Overture places. The methodology explains that the classification uses Census employer density and the app repeatedly warns about limits, but a nontechnical owner can still read “Gap” as a demand opportunity rather than a narrow two-axis classification.
- **Low — Spanish localization is incomplete.** Reproduce: ask the Utuado bakery question in Spanish. The core answer and business panel switch languages, but several map, search, microphone, talk, dismissal, and accessibility labels remain English.
- **Low — one place icon request fails.** Opening the Utuado bakery results produced a browser-console 404 for `https://icons.duckduckgo.com/ip3/lantiguabakery.com.ico`. The result card still rendered and the failure did not block the flow.
- **High — none observed.** All tested questions returned an answer, explicit refusal, or traceable limitation; I saw no crash, secret exposure, or fabricated claim presented without a source/qualification during the tested path.

## Scores (four criteria, 1-5 each, one sentence of evidence per score)

- **Intelligent and thoughtful use of LLMs + the Qloo API — 5/5.** The agent selects and traces narrow Qloo tools, adapts language and context, distinguishes model wording from rule-based numbers, and the catalog-only control demonstrates that affinity data changes the substance of answers.
- **Technical implementation and execution — 4/5.** The hosted 3D map, 78-town data joins, evidence drawers, bilingual live answers, source-level caveats, and public reproducibility story are substantial, but the mobile/state-coherence defect and incomplete localization keep it below exemplary execution.
- **Originality and creativity — 5/5.** Turning music and cultural taste affinities into an explorable Puerto Rico map and then combining them with Census and observed-supply signals for business siting is distinctive and memorable.
- **Real-world application and potential impact — 4/5.** It produces actionable scouting leads and honestly states what it cannot know, but it cannot measure demand, rents, foot traffic, permits, revenue, or profit, and its catalogs may be incomplete or stale.

## Top 3 changes that would raise the score, in order

1. Make town/query state atomic across desktop and mobile: a question naming San Juan should update the URL, composer context, map, and visible/detail panels together, dismiss stale overlays, and have a regression test at 390 px.
2. Turn business siting from a compelling lead generator into a decision workflow by adding current rents, permits/zoning, foot traffic or mobility proxies, closure/opening history, and an exportable side-by-side town scorecard; keep every new input sourced and uncertainty-bounded.
3. Add an ambiguity/clarification policy and finish Spanish localization: ask one short follow-up when category, town, or objective is unclear, and translate every visible and accessible control rather than only the generated content.

## What a rival entry would need to beat this

A rival would need more than a conversational wrapper around Qloo. It would need equally inspectable live tool traces and a fair with/without-Qloo control, while surpassing Zemi-Aku on decision quality: fresh ground truth, stronger causal restraint, complete multilingual/mobile coherence, and a closed loop from discovery to a saved trip or defensible business plan. The clearest winning move would be verified outcome data—showing that recommendations improve an actual travel choice or business scouting decision—without losing this entry's unusually candid source and limitation disclosures.
