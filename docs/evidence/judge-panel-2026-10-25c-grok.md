# Scores

**Intelligent and thoughtful use of LLMs and the Qloo API: 4.** The model is named on screen, tool calls are listed, a no-Qloo rerun removes the fan places, and the bakery-versus-restaurant split uses different Census counts and a different quadrant. The open-a-business path is a 0.5 s paraphrase of a heatmap saved on 11 October, and the flagship ablation credits the taste signal with one place, La Victoria.

**Technical implementation and execution: 4.** The map, the Spanish/English switch, the town panel, evidence, the scope guard for Miami, and the phone layout all ran. Document loads were under about one second. The earnings question took the wrong path, the card covers the map, and a commercial Cesium banner sits on the phone search box.

**Originality and creativity: 4.** Scoring all 78 towns as taste against published establishment counts, then showing the same model with the taste tools removed, is a specific use of Qloo rather than a chat box over a place list. The municipal "brecha" story is the idea. The artist tour is the demo on top of it.

**Real-world application and potential impact: 3.** A Spanish-speaking owner can get a sourced visit list and an honest "not enough data" for Maricao, with Google Maps links. The headline asks where she should open. Asked what she would earn, the app did not answer and did not say why. Stale "Hoy" hours and non-bakeries in the bakery list are what she would trust first.

# Top 3 changes that would raise the score, in order

1. If the question is about earnings, rent, sales, or profit, answer that sentence. The refusal already exists ("ninguna fuente aquí trae ingresos mensuales"). Put it on the plain question, in Spanish, in the card. Do not replace it with the gap paragraph.

2. Print the catalog date on every hours line, and stop saying "Hoy" unless the hours were read today. On an industry list, flag or drop places whose Qloo category is not that industry, the way the panel already explains NAICS versus Qloo in a paragraph she will not read.

3. Make Qloo change the town rank on a live click, and show that the way the fan-list control already shows place names. Right now the rank comes from a file dated 11 October, and the public ablation says the taste signal, as opposed to the catalog, changed one San Juan restaurant. A judge should be able to remove Qloo and watch Bayamón and San Juan trade places, or the app should say the rank is a saved heatmap and the live call is only the place list.

# What a rival entry would need to beat this

A live Qloo call whose removal changes which town is recommended, not only which three venues are named, rerun in the room rather than a transcript saved on 24 October. An answer, in Spanish, to "cuánto voy a ganar" that either cites a real proxy or refuses in the same breath, without a second prompt that begs it to lie. Hours and categories that match the claim on the card. A phone screen without a "Upgrade for commercial use" line across the question box.

This entry already has the 78-town join, the Census suppression rules, the named model, and a control that deletes the fan list. A prettier map will not beat that. A demo that still recommends La Placita with the taste tools off, and that cannot say "I don't know your profit," does not beat it either.
