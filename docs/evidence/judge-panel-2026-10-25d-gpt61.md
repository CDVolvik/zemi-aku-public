**B — Opened Evidence.** The expanded record showed artist_places for San Juan, Bad Bunny, food, returning five Qloo places in 0.5 seconds, followed by map_town. It reported a live model, two rounds, two tool calls, 2.6 seconds, a timestamp, and abbreviated Qloo IDs. This is better evidence than a marketing assertion of an agent. It remains a record presented by the application, not independent verification of the upstream API response or backend implementation.

**B — Comparison page.** /compare showed seven saved pairs, dated 2026-10-24, and disclosed that six remove Qloo's catalog and taste tools together. Those six demonstrate value from Qloo broadly, but do not isolate taste ranking. The last San Juan/Bad Bunny pair retains the catalog and removes affinity tools, a materially stronger control. Its own summary says the taste signal changed La Victoria; La Placita appears in both answers. I would not describe this as a dramatic wholesale transformation. **B — Different artist.** I asked “Where do Vico C fans eat in San Juan?” The first match changed from La Placita to Los Kings Lounge (96%), followed by El Techo (96%), La Victoria and La Placita (95%); Mesa 364 and El Callejón de la Tanca also appeared. This demonstrates differing scores/order and some membership changes beyond the required Bad Bunny example, though considerable overlap remains.

**B — Live ablation.** I clicked “Ask again without Qloo.” The completed answer said the artist-fan tool was off and “I can't build a real list of where Vico C fans eat. I won't guess.” It offered explicitly labeled general knowledge about Santurce and Río Piedras instead. The comparison named four places Qloo changed, and the control reported one call, 1.9 seconds. The pending state was clear: “Asking again without Qloo…” with a disabled button. However, reopening Details showed a general San Juan Qloo catalog with hotels, a mall and restaurants, while the breadcrumb still read “Vico C fans in San Juan.” This muddies what the control removed.

**B — Skeptical conclusion.** I observed no proof of fabricated venue data or a wholly scripted chat. Town-specific off-script answers, changing artist ranks, tool records and the live ablation support functional tool use. The business response structure is highly templated, and its wording/0.4–0.5-second timings alone cannot establish how much reasoning the model performs. Saved Qloo catalogs and dated heatmaps are disclosed; “live model” should not be mistaken for freshly fetched underlying business data. The app was responsive in these tests, with no user-facing answer failure. Initial map-load time was not independently measured, and transport/tool overhead makes precise wall-clock claims inappropriate.

## Defects (severity High/Med/Low, reproducible steps)

1. **Med — Control result and surrounding catalog disagree about scope.** Persona B: ask the Vico C/San Juan question, open Evidence, run without Qloo, then expand Details. The control says the prior mapped/listed places were removed, yet a Qloo-branded general catalog appears under the stale artist breadcrumb. The exact prior list is gone, but the remaining catalog can look like part of a supposedly Qloo-free result. Preserve a clearly labeled control state and separate background catalog data from control output.
2. **Med — Eating intent yields mostly nightlife, including a closed venue.** Persona B: submit the required Bad Bunny question. Several top matches are bars; El Techo says “Closed today.” The taxonomy explanation prevents concealment but does not solve the user's meal-selection problem. Distinguish restaurant affinity from verified food service and availability; offer a meal-focused filter.
3. **Med — Percentage labels invite a behavioral reading the evidence does not establish.** Persona B: inspect artist result cards such as “Strong match for Vico C fans (96%).” The visible record gives IDs and tool names but no immediate definition of what 96% means. Neither this test nor the comparison establishes that 96% of fans eat there. Pair the percentage with a plain-language score definition and avoid presenting aggregate affinity as observed patronage.
4. **Low — Ambiguous follow-up silently changes municipality.** Persona A: ask about a bakery in Adjuntas, then “¿Y allí?” The app selects the previously mentioned Río Grande without confirming the referent. Reasonable interpretation, but consequential enough for a short “¿Río Grande o Adjuntas?” choice.
5. **Low — Phone reading burden.** Persona A: use 390px width, obtain the Adjuntas/Río Grande business answers, and read prose, cards and Evidence. The nested answer scroller consumes much of the screen; the map and details compete for space. No horizontal overflow or complete interaction blocker was observed.
6. **Low — Snapshot hours look current in cards.** Both personas: cards say “Hoy…” or “Open 24 hours,” while business limitations elsewhere say hours are a dated catalog snapshot. This is disclosed, but the freshness warning is separated from the moment a user chooses a visit. Add the source date or “confirm hours” beside the schedule.
7. **Low — Unasked staffing assumption.** Persona B: request exact bakery profit in Adjuntas. The refusal ends by treating the user's operation as excluded from employee-establishment counts, although the user never said they would have no employees. Keep the exclusion conditional.

No High-severity defect was demonstrated. The preview-browser timeouts are excluded from product defects.

## Scores (four criteria, 1-5 each, one sentence of evidence per score)

- **Intelligent and thoughtful use of LLMs + Qloo API: 4/5.** Off-script artist results changed scores/order, live tool evidence was inspectable, and a live no-Qloo control lost the fan-specific recommendations, but score meaning and control-state clarity need work.
- **Technical implementation and execution: 4/5.** Spanish/English transitions, town and industry context, interactive map panels, evidence records and a usable 390px flow worked without an answer failure, with remaining control-state and mobile-reading defects.
- **Originality and creativity: 4/5.** Comparing municipal cultural affinity with employee-establishment density and suppression-aware Census counts is more distinctive than a generic restaurant chatbot, although artist-to-place discovery itself is familiar.
- **Real-world application and potential impact: 3/5.** The Spanish business flow supports concrete research visits and honest limitations, but it cannot validate demand, rents, foot traffic or profitability and no demonstrated user outcome establishes business impact.

## Top 3 changes that would raise the score, in order

1. Make the control indisputable: keep catalog access constant in more taste-only comparisons, clearly isolate every result/map layer during live ablation, fix stale breadcrumbs, and define affinity percentages alongside their source/date. This directly improves judge confidence in what Qloo contributes.
2. Turn research into a focused owner workflow: clarify ambiguous towns, show bakery comparisons side by side, keep the three next actions visible on phones, and attach date/verification status to hours and meal availability. Validate the flow with Spanish-first owners and report observed task completion.
3. Demonstrate real decision value with a small documented field study: verify competitor locations/hours, gather actual rent and foot-traffic observations for a few candidate areas, and show whether the affinity-plus-supply shortlist improves on a catalog-only shortlist. Maintain the current refusal to predict profits from affinity.

## What a rival entry would need to beat this

A rival needs more than a polished map or fluent restaurant prose. It should show equally accessible Spanish onboarding, genuinely different off-script Qloo results, inspectable tool use, a catalog-preserving taste ablation, and honest handling of missing data. To win decisively, it should add verified meal/business constraints, a simpler phone workflow, and evidence that actual owners or visitors make better choices with Qloo than with the same catalog alone. This entry's strongest defense is its concrete municipal data integration and unusually explicit limits; its opening is the gap between plausible affinity-based research and measured real-world usefulness.
