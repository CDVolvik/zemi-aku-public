# Blind evaluation: Zemi-Aku (https://zemi.r21digital.com)

Evaluator: Claude Opus 5.5, run 2026-10-25 13:40–13:47 UTC. I worked only from the public site and the public GitHub API, and read no project code or docs.

## Browser used

Playwright MCP (headless Chromium). The desktop viewport was about 960×900, and I later resized to 390×844 for the phone check. I inspected network requests and `/api/chat` response bodies through DevTools-style capture. Questions sent: 10 in total (2 from industry/town clicks, the scripted Bad Bunny question, 1 "Ask again without Qloo", and 6 of my own). I never touched the mic or the voice agent and never pressed playback. The app did fire `POST /api/speak` on its own after answers (requests #1099 and #1229), so TTS is fetched automatically. In headless mode I could not tell whether that audio actually played.

Load: `/app` returned HTTP 200 in 0.17 s from curl. With a warm cache, DOMContentLoaded came at about 0.24 s and pulled 250 resources. I did not measure a cold first load. Answer latency, as reported by the Evidence panel and consistent with what I saw: 0.4–5.9 s.

## Persona A walkthrough (PR bakery owner, Spanish first)

1. **Opened /app logged out.** The page loaded in Spanish: "¿Dónde abro un negocio en Puerto Rico?" / "El gusto de Qloo en los 78 pueblos, frente a los negocios que el Census ya cuenta. Escoja una industria." Industry chips appear in Spanish (Cafeterías, Panaderías, …). The search box placeholder stays in English: "Ask Zemi-Aku, or search a town or place".
2. **Clicked Panaderías.** A legend explains four groups: "Brecha: afinidad sobre la mediana, pocos negocios", "Competido…", "Tranquilo…", "Saturado…". Below it: "Más gusto y pocos negocios", with Río Grande "gusto #2 de 76 · 0.7 por cada 10,000 residentes", Luquillo "hasta 1.1", Fajardo, San Juan "#5 de 76 · 0.5", and others. The app openly drops three towns: "Vieques (gusto #1), Culebra (gusto #8), Maunabo (gusto #10) no están aquí: el Census no publica su conteo, así que no se sabe si tiene pocos negocios." That is honest. It also means the #1 taste town is never recommended.
3. **Clicked San Juan.** The panel shows "Lo que dicen los datos": "Brecha … gusto #5 de 76 … 16 establecimientos con empleados, 0.5 por cada 10,000 residentes frente a la mediana de 1.3 … Qloo registra 37 lugares de este tipo; Overture, 64. El Census cuenta solo panaderías al detal, con empleados (NAICS 311811)…". Next comes "Lo que no puede decir: Demanda, ventas, rentas, tráfico peatonal, permisos ni ganancias." Then "Tres pasos siguientes": visit named competitors at opening time, ask about rents and permits, and compare with the next gap town. Then come Overture supply (release 2026-09-23.1), Census PRCS (population 336,160, median household income $27,992 ± $1,036), and Qloo cultural affinities (music: Bad Bunny, Residente…; TV: "Lady of Steel, Exatlon…").
   - **Sources ("Fuentes y límites")** are clear and correctly hedged. They cite County Business Patterns 2023, explain that CBP suppresses counts under 3 ("tiene de 0 a 2"), state that the taste figure is the Qloo heatmap averaged over the town's cells as of 2026-10-11, cite PRCS 2020–2024, and add "Las clasificaciones e interpretaciones son de Zemi-Aku."
   - The spoken/answer bubble gave the same number in different units: "unos 5 por cada cien mil residentes frente a una mediana de 13". The panel beside it says 0.5 per 10,000 against 1.3. A non-technical reader will see "5 vs 13" and "0.5 vs 1.3" side by side. The panel also cites a second reference figure, "(isla, cifras publicadas: 0.8)", next to the 1.3 median.
   - On a ~960 px desktop viewport, the answer bubble overlapped the sources text in the left panel (screenshot taken).
4. **Changed to Restaurantes.** San Juan becomes "Competido … gusto #4 de 78 … 821 establecimientos … 24.4 por cada 10,000 frente a la mediana de 9.6". The first suggested competitor to visit is "La Placita de Santurce", which is a market plaza and nightlife district, not a restaurant.
5. **Off-script, Spanish: "Quiero abrir una panadería pero no tengo mucho dinero. ¿En qué pueblo me conviene?"** (5.9 s, 3 calls: `opportunity_map`, `find_places`, `map_town`). Answer: "…pone a Río Grande en el grupo brecha: 0.65 negocios por cada 10,000 residentes, por debajo de la mediana de 1.30… Alquileres, tráfico y ventas no están en estos datos; Qloo mide afinidad, no demanda." It named three Río Grande bakeries to visit. The budget part of the question was never addressed, beyond the general caveat that rents aren't in the data.
6. **Small town: "¿Y en Maricao? ¿Tiene sentido abrir una panadería allí?"** (0.4 s, labelled "investigación de negocio · fuentes + modelo en vivo"). Answer: "No hay datos suficientes para panaderías en Maricao: sin cifra publicada, de 0 a 2 establecimientos, y afinidad puesto 37 de 76. Qloo registra uno y otros datos del mapa uno." The last sentence is awkward Spanish. The Evidence panel shows a raw "· unknown" where a date should be (`opportunity_map bakery → … 78 towns · unknown`). This is the right answer in honest form, but it leaves out Maricao's population and income even though `census_prcs` was fetched.
7. **Ambiguous: "¿cuál es mejor?"** It resolved the question from context (Río Grande vs Maricao) without asking back: "Río Grande y Maricao no se pueden ordenar: una cifra retenida puede estar por debajo o por encima… La cifra más clara es la de Río Grande…". That is reasonable, but "cifra retenida" and "brecha" are jargon for this persona.
8. **"¿Qué tipo de clientes van a Panadería Don Nico en Río Grande?"** Answer: **"No hay datos sobre el tipo de clientes de Panadería Don Nico. Qloo no guarda esa información, ni edades ni perfiles."** This is false. When I opened Don Nico's own card, "Audience and taste map → Who likes it · Qloo" showed age bands (24 and under above average, 30–34 below, …) and gender (women below, men above), with the note "Compared with Qloo's average audience. Not a customer count." The agent called `town_catalog` and `find_places` but never the audience tool, then denied the data exists. See Defect H1.
9. **"Dime cuánto vende al año Panadería Don Nico y cuánto cuesta la renta…"** It refused cleanly with 0 tool calls: "No tengo esas cifras… nada de ingresos ni precios de alquiler. Para eso conviene preguntar directo al negocio y a un corredor de bienes raíces local." Small overstatement: PRCS household income is in the data.
10. **Place card in Spanish mode.** The chrome is Spanish, but the content is English: "Neighborhood bakery and casual eatery…", "Its fans also love", "See in 3D", "stars", "Contact and sources". It also showed a generic bread illustration instead of a photo.

## Persona B walkthrough (judge, 10 minutes)

1. **Is the agent real?** Yes, as far as I can test from outside. Each `/api/chat` response carries an `evidence` object with model, rounds, ms and per-tool calls with args and Qloo entity ids. For the scripted question it showed `{"tool":"artist_places","args":{"town":"San Juan","artist":"Bad Bunny","category":"food"},"source":"Qloo","count":5,"ids":[…]}`, using model `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` via OpenRouter in 2 rounds over 1.9 s. Different questions picked different tools (`opportunity_map`, `find_places`, `town_catalog`, `town_profile`), and the refusal question made 0 calls. That looks like genuine tool routing, not a script. I could not verify that the 384 ms `artist_places` call hit Qloo live rather than a cache, and the landing page says Qloo answers are cached.
2. **Scripted question, "Where do Bad Bunny fans eat in San Juan?"** The UI switched to English (header "Puerto Rico, through Qloo's taste data", voice chip "EN · Andromeda"). Answer: "…lean toward La Placita de Santurce, La Victoria on Calle Loíza, and Los Kings Lounge… around 87 to 88 percent. Note this is Qloo taste data: these are places fans like, not spots tied to Bad Bunny." The five cards were genres Bar, Bar, Fast food restaurant, Restaurant and **Gay bar** (Oasis Tapas & Lounge), all filed under "Food". For "eat", 3 of the 5 are bars. Each card says "Why it fits you · Strong match for Bad Bunny fans (88%)". Presenting the 0.88 affinity as a percentage invites a probability reading, and "you" is wrong because the user asked about fans.
3. **Evidence panel** content: "Evidence · live model · 1 tool call · 1.9 s / artist_places San Juan, Bad Bunny, food → Qloo · 5 places · 0.4 s / Model … 2 rounds / Ask again without Qloo / Qloo IDs: FFE3…12E5, …". It is useful but thin. There is no raw Qloo response, affinity values, or request URL in the UI; I needed DevTools to see them.
4. **"Ask again without Qloo"** ran live (2.9 s, 3 calls). Evidence: "artist_places → refused: Unknown tool or invalid arguments / map_town San Juan → done / landmarks San Juan → Wikidata". So the control model really lacked the tool. Its answer, though, misdescribed what happened: "**The Qloo place lookup for Bad Bunny fans didn't return anything in San Juan**". It wasn't empty; the tool was refused. It then named La Placita and Calle Loíza "from general knowledge only… not data", which is properly labelled. The diff line read "Qloo changed: La Victoria, Los Kings Lounge, El Techo Rooftop Bar". So the honest finding is that Qloo's marginal effect was 3 named venues, while the headline venue (La Placita) appears either way.
5. **/compare** is a static saved run ("run 2026-10-24 22:24 UTC", "made by scripts/compare-qloo.mjs against a local server"), not a live run. It has 7 pairs with the same model, prompt and settings. The best-designed pair is the last: catalog-only versus catalog+affinity for the Bad Bunny question. It shows **"Qloo's taste signal changed: La Victoria"**, so the affinity signal changed one venue out of three. The page states this plainly, which is creditable and also an admission that the taste signal's effect is modest there. Other pairs show the no-Qloo model refusing to name places ("I won't invent one"). That proves grounding discipline more than it proves Qloo's value, because the control is told it has no places. In the mofongo pair, the with-Qloo run admits "no hay coincidencia estricta" (`find_places … mofongo → 0 places`) and falls back to "La Guarina Bakery" for "comida boricua casual", which is a weak fallback. The Calle 13 "which town" question called `artist_places` for only 4 towns the model chose (one "dropped to fit the request"), so it is not a 78-town ranking. Small inconsistency: San Juan food catalog is "942 places" on /compare and "941 lugares" in the app.
6. **Things the app admits** (many, all to its credit): "Lugares registrados por Qloo, no negocios confirmados en venta. Afinidad, demografía y oferta observada no prueban demanda ni rentabilidad." / "Lo que no puede decir: Demanda, ventas, rentas…" / CBP suppression explained / "Pista, no prueba" / "Compared with Qloo's average audience. Not a customer count." / "Qloo audience connections, not the place's own playlist." / "affinities, not endorsements" on the landing page / "Datos de gusto de la API de Qloo (acceso del hackathon)" / "a place the second run names from general knowledge is not verified" on /compare.
7. **Anything faked?** I found no fabricated places; every named venue traced to a Qloo id or an Overture record. Things that look hard-coded or pre-computed: the opportunity map is a batch read ("leído 2026-10-11 07:13 UTC"), town catalogs are dated 2026-10-07, and /compare is saved. The landing page claims "a note that restates a figure is dropped", yet the live answer restated "around 87 to 88 percent" and "0.65 … mediana de 1.30".
8. **Public repo**: the GitHub API reports public, MIT, JavaScript, last pushed 2026-10-25 13:41 UTC. I did not read its code, per the brief.

## Scores

1. **Intelligent use of LLMs + Qloo API: 4/5.** Tool choice varies sensibly by question, the Evidence trace shows real Qloo calls with entity ids, and the on-demand "without Qloo" ablation is a genuinely thoughtful control. It loses a point because the agent denied audience data that Qloo supplies (H1), and the saved catalog-only control shows the affinity signal changed only 1 of 3 venues.
2. **Technical implementation and execution: 4/5.** It is fast (0.4–5.9 s per answer, 0.17 s page response), has no functional errors in 10 questions, ties the map, panel and chat state together, keeps state in URL hashes (`#town=…&place=<qloo id>`), and the phone layout works. It loses a point for language leakage, a raw "unknown", panel overlap and a control-run misstatement.
3. **Originality and creativity: 4/5.** Crossing Qloo taste heatmaps with Census CBP supply into Gap/Competitive/Quiet/Crowded quadrants, with suppression-aware grouping, is an original and well-reasoned use of a taste API. The map-plus-agent shell and artist-to-places discovery are less novel.
4. **Real-world application and impact: 3/5.** The honesty about limits is exemplary ("No mide demanda, ingresos ni ganancias"), and the next steps are practical. But for the stated user, a Spanish-first non-technical bakery owner, it falls short in three ways. The core signal (averaged taste affinity) is not demand. Budget isn't addressed. The answers lean on jargon and mixed languages. And one factual denial (H1) would mislead exactly the question she would ask.

## Top 3 changes that would raise the score, in order

1. **Fix H1 and make the agent use, or at least acknowledge, every Qloo tool the UI exposes.** When asked "who goes to X", call the audience tool and say "Qloo compares its audience to its own average: more under-25s and men than average; this is not a customer count." Add a guard so the model can't claim a field is absent when the card shows it.
2. **Make the control run honest and visible as evidence of Qloo's value.** Fix the "didn't return anything" misstatement, put the catalog-only vs affinity diff live in /app instead of only on the saved /compare page, and show raw affinity values and the Qloo request in Evidence without DevTools.
3. **Finish Persona A's experience.** Keep the whole UI in Spanish, including place descriptions (translate or label "texto de Qloo, en inglés"). Use one density unit everywhere. Gloss "brecha" in plain words ("mucho gusto por panaderías y pocas panaderías"). Use PRCS income when the user mentions money. Map "eat" to restaurant genres instead of bars.

## What a rival entry would need to beat this

A rival would need Qloo to visibly drive a decision, not just a list. That means a live A/B in the main flow where the taste signal reorders towns or venues, and the difference is shown and explained. It would also need the same refusal-to-fabricate discipline Zemi-Aku already has, which is its strongest asset and hard to match. Beating it outright would take one of two things: a demand proxy layered on taste (foot traffic, search interest, or Qloo trends over time) so the answer goes beyond "taste vs supply", or a tighter non-technical UX in one language with no factual slips like H1. A flashier entry without Zemi-Aku's sourcing and ablation would not beat it with a skeptical judge.
