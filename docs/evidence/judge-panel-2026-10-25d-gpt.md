## Browser used

Chromium through Playwright CLI, logged out, on the public deployment at `https://zemi.r21digital.com`. I tested the desktop flow and resized the viewport to 390 × 844 CSS pixels. I submitted five normal questions plus one explicit no-Qloo control rerun; I did not use the microphone, voice agent, or speech playback. The initial map and panels loaded without a user-facing error. Recorded answer times in the app's own Evidence panels were 0.9–2.5 seconds for ordinary successful prompts, 9.3 seconds for a seven-tool-call partial failure, and 1.4 seconds for the no-Qloo control.

## Persona A walkthrough (what you did, what you saw, quotes of on-screen text)

**Persona A: Puerto Rico small-business owner, nontechnical, Spanish-first.** I opened `/app` logged out. The landing screen was immediately understandable but English-first: “Where should I open a business in Puerto Rico?” with large industry choices. I chose **Bakeries**, then **San Juan** from “Strongest taste with few businesses.” The app classified San Juan as a “Gap” and gave a concrete comparison: “taste #5 of 76,” 16 employee establishments, 0.5 per 10,000 residents, and an island grouping median of 1.3. It also named bakeries to visit and offered directions.

The decision support was unusually candid. On the San Juan panel it said: “Places recorded by Qloo, not confirmed businesses for sale. Affinities, demographics and observed supply are not proof of demand or profitability.” Under “What it cannot tell you,” it listed “Demand, sales, rents, foot traffic, permits or profit.” The expanded sources identified County Business Patterns 2023, Puerto Rico Community Survey 2020–2024, a Qloo bakery heatmap retrieved 2026-10-11, and Overture release 2026-09-23.1. It explained Census suppression below three establishments and warned that Qloo/Overture place lists and the Census establishment count measure different things. This is the right level of caution for a real lease decision.

I then asked in Spanish, “¿Dónde abrirías una cafetería en Maricao y por qué?” The entire interface switched to Spanish without setup. The answer said the Census count was suppressed (therefore 0–2 cafés), coffee affinity was high at rank 8 of 78, and explicitly warned: “Rentas y tránsito peatonal no están en los datos.” It named two existing businesses to inspect. However, it still asserted “Abriría cerca del centro, junto a la plaza, donde ya hay movimiento,” even though it had no foot-traffic evidence; that recommendation sounds more certain than its data allows.

For an intentionally vague Spanish prompt, “¿Dónde está bueno?”, it did not bluff. It replied: “Maricao es pequeño y la pregunta me queda amplia. ¿Busca comida, vida nocturna, playas y aire libre, cultura, o un negocio para abrir?” The Evidence panel showed zero tool calls and 0.9 seconds. This was a good clarification behavior for a nontechnical user.

At 390 px, the map, two-column place cards, scrollable detail sheet, search bar, language control, and evidence/answer controls remained usable. The sheet occupies most of the screen and the transient answer is visually truncated, but a clear “Show the answer” control preserves access. The Spanish-first user still has to discover Spanish by typing it or using the compact `EN` control; the first impression is English.

## Persona B walkthrough

**Persona B: hackathon judge checking whether the agent and Qloo integration are real.** After changing the San Juan business category to **Restaurants**, I asked “Where do Bad Bunny fans eat in San Juan?” The answer named La Placita de Santurce, La Victoria, Los Kings Lounge, El Techo Rooftop Bar, and Oasis Tapas & Lounge, with displayed affinities from 86% to 88%. It carefully said these were “taste affinities, not Bad Bunny's own spots.” It also admitted a taxonomy mismatch: “Qloo lists these bars under restaurants, which is why they appear here.”

The expanded Evidence panel was concrete rather than decorative. It showed the `artist_places` tool invocation (“San Juan, Bad Bunny, food → Qloo · 5 places · 0.2 s”), the live model (`deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` via OpenRouter), two rounds, the answer timestamp, truncated Qloo IDs for all five places, and an “Ask again without Qloo” button. On a repeat run at mobile width, Evidence showed two tool calls in 2.2 seconds (`map_town` and `artist_places`). The no-Qloo button then produced a 1.4-second control with “without Qloo tools, no history.” It refused to claim fan-affinity data and the UI summarized: “Qloo changed: La Placita de Santurce, La Victoria, El Techo Rooftop Bar”; those mapped/listed places were removed from the control result. This is strong evidence that the agent is executing tools and that Qloo changes the output.

I opened `/compare`. It disclosed seven saved pairs, the exact model and settings, the tools available in each condition, run time, tool traces, catalog counts, and a sentence stating what Qloo changed. The strongest experiment is the final San Juan pair: both sides retain the Qloo catalog, while only the first has affinity tools. That isolates taste signal from mere catalog access. The page also reveals mixed effect sizes rather than claiming universal improvement: in that pair, the affinity signal changed only La Victoria, while some general nightlife knowledge survived without affinity. The controls are saved runs made against a local server, not themselves live, but the live per-answer no-Qloo control resolves that credibility concern.

For an off-script stress test I asked, “I like Björk. Which town in Puerto Rico fits my taste?” The agent spent 9.3 seconds across seven tool calls, then openly said: “I could not rank Puerto Rico towns by Björk taste this run; that Qloo request came back unanswered.” It nevertheless fell back to the already-open town and retitled the panel “Björk fans in Maricao,” showing four local affinity matches (67%–73%). The disclosure is good, but the stateful fallback does not answer the requested island-wide comparison and could make the current town look selected by evidence when it was merely already open.

No response looked like a hidden static script: wording varied between repeated Bad Bunny runs, evidence showed different tool counts/timings, the ambiguous prompt used no tools, Spanish changed the application locale, and the Björk request exposed a real partial failure. The business workflow itself appears more deterministic/precomputed than the conversational agent, but it gives dates, methodology, source boundaries, and a distinct live-model Evidence record rather than pretending all content is generated live.

## Scores (four criteria, 1-5 each, one sentence of evidence per score)

- **Intelligent and thoughtful use of LLMs + the Qloo API — 5/5.** The live agent selects tools, clarifies ambiguity, changes locale, exposes partial failures, and provides a same-prompt no-Qloo control with tool traces and Qloo IDs that demonstrates causal output changes.
- **Technical implementation and execution — 4/5.** The 3D map, sourced business analysis, live multi-round tool use, evidence UI, paired controls, and responsive layout work well, but state leakage after the failed Björk ranking and category/hour ambiguity keep it below flawless.
- **Originality and creativity — 5/5.** Combining cultural affinity heatmaps with Census supply, town-level geography, conversational exploration, and an embedded ablation experiment is distinctive and unusually judgeable.
- **Real-world application and potential impact — 4/5.** It is a credible discovery and hypothesis-generation tool for local businesses and visitors, but the app itself correctly acknowledges that it lacks the demand, rent, foot-traffic, permit, sales, and profit data needed for an investment decision.

## Top 3 changes that would raise the score, in order

1. **Enforce an evidence boundary on business recommendations.** If the data is town-level, recommend a town and a field-research plan; do not choose “near the plaza” or claim movement unless a cited pedestrian, commercial-rent, zoning, or permit source supports it.
2. **Make category and entity semantics stricter.** Separate restaurants from nightlife, distinguish districts/markets from individual venues, suppress unsupported venue hours, and let the user explicitly include bars when asking where to eat.
3. **Handle failed comparative queries without contextual bias.** When island-wide ranking fails, keep the result in a neutral error/clarification state and offer “show matches in the currently open town” as an explicit fallback rather than automatically relabeling that town as the answer context.

## What a rival entry would need to beat this

A rival would need more than a polished chatbot or a list of Qloo recommendations. It would need equally inspectable live tool execution, honest source/limitation language, and a reproducible with/without-Qloo experiment, while adding stronger commercial ground truth—current menus and hours, rents, foot traffic, permits, and demand—and using those sources to produce recommendations at the geographic precision it claims. It should also preserve this entry's fast bilingual clarification and mobile usability, but avoid category leakage and current-town bias. In short, the winning rival would have to match Zemi-Aku's transparency and originality while turning its strong lead-generation evidence into defensible decision evidence.
