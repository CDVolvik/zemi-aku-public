What Qloo brings

Take Qloo away and the places go with it.

Every place on the map comes from Qloo, and so does every taste it can search and every "who likes it" chart. Zemi-Aku's job is to ask Qloo the right question for the town on screen, then say only what comes back. Seven kinds of call do the work. The with-and-without-Qloo page runs seven questions through the same model twice, once without the Qloo tools, and shows what changed and where Qloo added nothing. Without Qloo the model still has weather, Wikidata and Wikipedia; it says it is answering from general knowledge and refuses to name places.

For travelers, the same calls power taste-led trips: artist affinities surface fan-favored cafés and bars (not celebrity endorsements), tag filters turn "mofongo" or "surf" into verified pins, and heatmaps show where a taste gathers before you commit to a town. Business and traveler journeys share one catalog — opening a bakery and planning from Bad Bunny both start from Qloo's 7,717 places.

The product

A god's-eye map you can talk to.

Pick a town, type a question or press the orb and speak it. The map flies there and the town opens to everything Qloo knows in it, sorted into categories: Ponce has 240 places to eat, 47 to shop and 16 to stay. Every business opens to the facts Qloo holds on it, including who likes it.

How it works

Ask, read, answer. Every place comes from a record.

The agent answers from tool results, not memory: it reads Qloo records for the town on screen and names only places they hold. The siting verdict is rule-based, from the saved map; the model writes the closing note and never the numbers. When Qloo knows a town only a little, it says so, and a run with the Qloo tools off says it is answering from general knowledge.

Ask

Type, or talk in English, Spanish, French, Italian or German. Town names are passed to the speech model so "Mayagüez" stays Mayagüez.

/api/askvoice

Read

The server pages through every place Qloo holds for the town, turns words like "surf" or "mofongo" into Qloo taste tags, and pulls a business's audience and footprint.

insightsfind_tagsaudience

Answer

The answer names the places it used. The voice speaks it, the map flies to them, and the record behind every answer is one click away.

evidencemap fly

Every place and every count traces to a Qloo record or an open-data join with its source; what the model adds is labeled as the model's, and a note that restates a figure is dropped.

What we measured

Things Qloo's Puerto Rico data taught us.

Each one changed the build. The evidence line is the measurement behind it.

Never look a place up by name.

"Plaza Las Americas" by name returned the malls in Ecatepec and Cancún. Every follow-up call uses Qloo's own id instead.qloo_describe, 7 Oct: two Mexico candidates, no Puerto Rico match

The printed city is often a barrio.

La Muralla China prints Coto Laurel as its city. Qloo's admin2_region says Ponce. Read the wrong field and a Ponce restaurant becomes a neighbor's.geocode.city "Coto Laurel" · geocode.admin2_region "Ponce"

A dish needs a filter, not a signal.

Asked to rank by mofongo, Qloo returned a supermarket and a Mexican restaurant. Filtered to places tagged mofongo, it returned six that serve it.signal: SuperMax, Lolita's · filter.tags: Cayo Caribe, Tayzan, Rompeolas…

Search in the language of the sign.

Puerto Rico names its businesses in Spanish. Near Caguas, "farmacia" finds 13 places and "pharmacy" finds none, so the search uses the words as spoken./search within 12 miles of Caguas, 7 Oct

A five-place sample hid most of the island.

Sampling five places a town, eight towns looked thin. Paging through everything Qloo holds told a different story: Cabo Rojo has 169 places and Toa Alta 63, and of the eight only Ceiba, with 15, is small. The map now counts every place. Eleven towns have fewer than 25, and the agent says so when you open one.Every page of /v2/insights for all 78 towns, 7 Oct 2026: 7,717 places. San Juan alone: 1,353 over 37 pages.

Limits

What it does not claim.

  • It is not a census. A town's catalog is every place Qloo holds there, not every business that exists.
  • Popularity and audience are Qloo's aggregate signals, not foot traffic or customers.
  • Affinity is not a visit count, a review score, or proof you will like a place — it is Qloo's taste signal against its average audience.
  • In thin towns some places belong to the next town over; each card says where it is. Eleven municipalities have fewer than 25 places on Qloo.
  • Hours and contact lines can be stale in Qloo's feed; check before you go, especially for holidays.
  • French, Italian and German are answered and spoken by the agent (Deepgram Aura-2 voices, added October 14); the rule-based fallback writes English and Spanish only.
